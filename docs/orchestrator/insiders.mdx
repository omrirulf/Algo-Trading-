title: "Insider Activity"
description: "Form 4 buying and selling — the one input where someone with better information spent their own money"
---

The fifth context source, and the one where the naive reading is most often
wrong. It answers a question none of the others can: has anyone who actually
runs this company just backed their view with their own cash?

It comes from yfinance, like the other three enrichment sources — **no API key
and no per-request cost.** Earlier drafts of this project assumed insider data
would need a paid provider; yfinance 0.2.44 exposes `insider_purchases`,
`insider_transactions` and `insider_roster_holders` directly.

## Buys and sells are not symmetric

This is the whole design.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="A purchase is a view" icon="arrow-trend-up">
    An insider buying on the open market is spending their own money on a
    company they understand better than you do. Several insiders buying
    independently, or an operating executive rather than a director, is
    stronger still.
  </Card>
  <Card title="A sale is mostly noise" icon="arrow-trend-down">
    Insiders sell for reasons that have nothing to do with the business: a
    scheduled 10b5-1 plan, tax on vesting shares, a house, plain
    diversification. A large sale often says nothing at all.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

The rendered block says this in the prompt itself, so the model cannot read a
routine sale as a bearish signal:

```
INSIDER ACTIVITY
Last 180 days: bought 120,000 shares in 4 transaction(s) | sold 900,000 shares in 12
Net: -780,000 shares (-2.1% of insider holdings) | insiders hold 37,000,000 shares
Distinct insiders: 1 buying, 1 selling
Open-market purchases — insiders spending their own money:
  - 2026-08-14 Jane Roe (CFO): 50,000 shares, 6.10M
  - 2026-07-02 Jane Roe (CFO): 20,000 shares, 2.40M
Sales — weak evidence on their own; often scheduled 10b5-1 plans, tax on vesting, or diversification:
  - 2026-09-01 John Doe (Director): 400,000 shares, 52.00M
(1 grant/option/gift transaction(s) excluded — compensation, not a view on the price)
```

## A grant is not a purchase

<Warning>
  Yahoo's transaction list mixes open-market purchases with stock awards,
  option exercises, gifts and tax withholding. Counting a grant as "insider
  buying" would turn routine compensation into a bullish signal and make the
  whole dimension noise.
</Warning>

Only genuine purchases and sales are counted. Everything else is tallied
separately and reported as excluded, so the exclusion is visible rather than
silent. `test_insiders.py` asserts this for stock awards, option exercises,
gifts and tax-liability payments individually.

## Distinct insiders, not transactions

One executive buying twice is *one* person with a view, not two. The summary
counts distinct names on each side, because three different insiders buying is
a materially different fact from one insider buying three times.

## Nothing happening is not nothing available

An empty transaction list and a failed fetch would look identical if both
rendered as silence, so they are kept apart:

| Situation | What the prompt says |
|---|---|
| Source returned no transactions | `No insider transactions reported in the last 180 days.` |
| Source failed | `INSIDER ACTIVITY` → `unavailable this cycle`, plus a named gap |

The model is told in the system prompt to treat "no transactions" as exactly
that, rather than as bearish.

## Windowing

Individual transactions are filtered to the last 180 days — matching the six
months yfinance's own rollup covers — and shown newest first, capped at four
per side. `WINDOW_DAYS` is a module constant.

## Where it sits in the pipeline

Insider data changes daily at most, so it rides the existing six-hour cache in
`context.py` alongside fundamentals and the analyst view. Unlike those two it
does **not** come from the `info` dict, so it is built independently: a ticker
whose `info` fetch failed can still have an insider section.

<Note>
  If the scorer shows this dimension carries signal but yfinance's version is
  too thin or too stale, the upgrade path is SEC EDGAR — Form 4 filings at the
  source, free and authoritative, needing only a User-Agent header. That is
  real engineering (CIK mapping, XML parsing), which is why it was not the
  starting point: [scoring the journal](/scoring) can now decide whether it is
  worth it.
</Note>
