title: "Market Context"
description: "Technicals, fundamentals, the analyst view and insider activity — the four sources that turn a headline reader into an analyst"

News alone tells the model that something happened. It does not tell it
whether the thing that happened landed on a cheap business or an expensive
one, on an uptrend or a breakdown, or on a name the street already loves.
`orchestrator/context.py` adds four more dimensions before the prompt is
built.

All four come from [yfinance](https://github.com/ranaroussi/yfinance), which
is unauthenticated — **the enriched context needs no new API key and adds no
per-request cost.**

## What goes into the prompt

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Technicals — orchestrator/technicals.py" icon="chart-line">
    Two years of daily bars, reduced to positioning and timing:

    - 20 / 50 / 200-day simple moving averages, the distance from each, and
      whether the 50 sits above or below the 200
    - Wilder RSI(14) and MACD (12/26/9) with its signal line and histogram
    - Trailing returns over 1 day, 5 days, 1 month and 3 months
    - 52-week high and low, and where the price sits inside that range
    - ATR(14) as a percentage of price, and annualised 20-day volatility
    - Latest volume as a multiple of its 20-day average

    The ATR is computed by `app.market_data.calculate_atr` — the *same*
    function the risk engine will use to place the stop, rather than a second
    implementation that could drift from it.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Fundamentals — orchestrator/fundamentals.py" icon="building-columns">
    Sector and industry, market cap, trailing and forward P/E, P/B, PEG,
    profit and operating margins, ROE, year-over-year revenue and earnings
    growth, debt/equity, free cash flow, beta, short interest as a percentage
    of float, and the next earnings date.

    Fields whose units yfinance has changed between releases are deliberately
    **not** carried. A number the prompt cannot label correctly is worse than
    no number: the model cannot tell a 0.45% dividend yield from a 45% one,
    and will happily build a thesis on the wrong one.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Analyst & institutional view — orchestrator/analysts.py" icon="users">
    What professional money says:

    - Consensus rating and its 1-to-5 mean, with the analyst count
    - The full rating breakdown (strong buy / buy / hold / sell / strong sell)
    - Mean, high and low price targets, and the implied upside against the
      last close
    - Recent upgrades and downgrades, by firm, with the grade moved from and to
    - Institutional ownership and the largest holders

    The prompt states the scale explicitly (`1=strong buy to 5=strong sell`),
    because a bare `2.05` is meaningless without knowing which end is bullish.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Insider activity — orchestrator/insiders.py" icon="user-secret">
    The six-month buy/sell rollup, net shares, distinct buyers versus sellers,
    and recent open-market purchases and sales by name and role.

    Unlike the other three, this one carries a built-in asymmetry: a purchase
    is someone spending their own money on a view, a sale is weak evidence,
    and a stock grant is not buying at all. Full treatment in
    [Insider Activity](/orchestrator/insiders).
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## How the model is told to weigh them

More data is only an improvement if it does not simply make the model more
confident. The system prompt is explicit that it must not:

> More context does not mean more conviction. Conviction is earned when
> independent dimensions agree, and it must fall when they conflict. Bullish
> news on a technically broken, richly valued name is a low-conviction signal,
> not a high one.

Each source also gets its own weighting instruction — news is fast and noisy,
technicals are about timing rather than quality, fundamentals rarely change a
view within an hour, the analyst view is a prior that is already in the price
unless it just moved, and insider buying counts for far more than insider
selling.

## Failures degrade, they don't propagate

Every source is fetched independently, and a failure records a *gap* instead
of raising. `gather()` never raises.

```
DATA GAPS (score these dimensions 0.0 rather than guessing)
- analyst recommendations unavailable: HTTP 429 Too Many Requests
```

The gaps are named in the prompt, and the model is told to score those
dimensions `0.0` rather than infer what the missing section would have said.
Failure text is trimmed to 120 characters first — five quoted connection
errors would otherwise cost more prompt tokens than the news does.

<Warning>
  News fails differently, and on purpose. If the enrichment sources are down
  the cycle continues on what is left; if **news** is down the ticker is
  skipped entirely. Silently trading on technicals alone would be running a
  strategy nobody signed off on.
</Warning>

## Caching

Fundamentals, analyst ratings, ownership and insider filings change daily at
most, and the heartbeat now runs daily. They are still cached for six hours per ticker
(`SLOW_DATA_TTL_SECONDS`); price history is refetched every cycle. Without
the cache a three-ticker watchlist would ask an unauthenticated, rate-limited
source the same question 24 times a day.

## No credentials, structurally

None of the context modules reads a setting, a key or a token, and CI greps
them to keep it that way. The check names the three modules that *may* hold a
credential and tests everything else in `orchestrator/`, so a context source
added later is covered automatically:

```bash
grep -rnE '(get_settings|os\.environ|getenv|\bsettings\.|\.[A-Za-z_]*(secret|api_key|_token|password)\b)' \
  orchestrator --include='*.py' \
  | grep -vE '^orchestrator/(heartbeat|news|llm)\.py:'
```

## Everything is testable without the network

`technicals.py`, `fundamentals.py`, `analysts.py` and `insiders.py` are pure
parsing: they take a DataFrame or a dict and return plain data. `context.py`
is the only module that touches yfinance, and it takes an injectable provider,
so the whole pipeline — including every degradation path — is exercised in
tests with no network access at all.
