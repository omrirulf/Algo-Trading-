title: "Currency risk"
description: "The account is in dollars. Your life is in shekels. That gap is a position."
---

Every number in this system is a dollar number. The journal, the scorer, the
backtest and the position caps all count dollars, so a move in USD/ILS does not
*reduce* a return — it silently **redenominates** one.

<Warning>
  **A 10% year in dollars, alongside a 10% fall in the dollar, is a −1% year in
  shekels.** Not break-even: the two compound. And a portfolio that traded
  nothing at all still moves in shekels, because holding dollars is a position.
</Warning>

```python
from orchestrator.fx import shekel_return_pct

shekel_return_pct(10, -10)   # -1.0
shekel_return_pct(0, -8)     # -8.0   <- the flat year that wasn't
```

## This cannot be hedged from here

Not "we chose not to" — there is no route from an Alpaca equities account to a
shekel:

| Instrument | Why it does not work |
|---|---|
| Spot FX, forwards, futures | Alpaca trades `us_equity`, `us_option` and `crypto` only — read off the SDK's own `AssetClass` enum. |
| A shekel currency ETF | **None exists.** US-listed single-currency funds cover the majors — `FXE` euro, `FXY` yen, `FXB` sterling — and the dollar index (`UUP`, `UDN`). The shekel is not among them. |
| "Currency-hedged" ETFs (`HEFA`, `DBEF`) | These hedge a **foreign** currency back into dollars — removing EUR or JPY exposure *for a dollar-based holder*. That is the opposite direction to what you need. |
| `UDN` as a proxy | Its basket is 58% euro and contains no shekel. Hedging USD/ILS with it is a bet on the euro wearing a hedge's clothing. |

<Note>
  **A partial hedge against the wrong index is a second position, not less
  risk.** A ~60%-correlated instrument with its own drift and its own costs
  adds a bet while leaving most of the basis risk in place. That is why this
  module measures instead.
</Note>

### The one natural offset, and its size

`EIS` holds Israeli equities that are themselves priced in shekels, so it
carries **embedded ILS exposure**: when the shekel strengthens, its dollar
value rises, all else equal.

But size it honestly. `EIS` is a broad fund capped at 12%, inside an
International equity group capped at 25%. Even at a full position that
is 12% of the book with embedded shekel exposure, against **100%** of
the account denominated in dollars. It is a rounding error on the problem, not
a hedge.

Gold (`GLD`) is a partial dollar-debasement hedge for similar reasons and with
the same caveat: it is not a shekel.

## What this system does instead

`orchestrator/fx.py` fetches USD/ILS once per cycle and **journals it on every
signal line**:

```json
"fx": {"symbol": "ILS=X", "rate": 3.7412, "change_3mo_pct": -2.3, "gap": ""}
```

Recorded at signal time on purpose. Without the rate *as of the entry*, no
later analysis can redenominate a trade — you would be converting last year's
P&L at today's rate, which is a different and wrong number.

A failed fetch degrades to a named gap and a `null` rate, never `0.0`. A zero
would read as "the shekel is worthless" rather than "we don't know".

<Note>
  The `market checks` workflow reports the current rate and flags it if the
  pair stops resolving — the journal would otherwise lose the only record of
  what trades were worth in the currency that matters, and lose it quietly.
</Note>

## Where the hedge actually belongs

Outside this account, where the instruments exist:

- **An FX forward or option at your bank**, sized against the account's dollar
  value. This is the direct answer and the only complete one.
- **Holding fewer dollars.** The account now runs close to fully invested
  (95%), so there is little idle cash left to be exposed — but that
  cuts both ways: the 95% that *is* invested is still denominated in
  dollars. Being fully invested changes what you own, not what currency you
  own it in.
- **Shekel-denominated assets held elsewhere**, sized against this account
  rather than in addition to it.

This page exists so that decision is made against a measured number instead of
a feeling.
