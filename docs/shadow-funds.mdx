The [horse race](/horse-race) judges each arm's calls one trade at a time:
three sessions each, no portfolio, no cash. It cannot say what an arm does
with a whole book — the sizing, the stops, the profit ladder, the group caps
and the cash all interacting. The shadow funds do.

**Simulated, not real money.** Nothing in `shadow/` can trade. CI checks that
it cannot construct a broker client, read a credential, reach the dispatcher
or write a file.

## The funds

| Fund | Signals | Run by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| model | the journal's own answers | the production `ExecutionEngine` and `PositionManager` |
| momentum | `rules/momentum.py` on each line's technicals | the same |
| hybrid | `rules/hybrid.py` on each line's technicals and news score | the same |
| VT | bought once at the first open with all the cash, then held | outside the engine |
| 1,000 coin-flip funds | `rules/control.py`, seeds 0 to 999 | the same as the model fund |

Each starts with $100,000. VT stays out of the engine on purpose: the engine
would size it as one equity at 5% of the book and put a stop under it, which
is not "hold the index".

## What is real and what is simulated

- **Real, unchanged**: the engine (conviction floor, position count, gross,
  sleeve, group and stock-market limits, ATR stop, sizing), the position
  manager (profit ladder, trailing stop, group-cap trims, protection), the
  ATR method, the audit record format and the parser that reads it back.
- **Simulated**: the broker (`shadow/broker.py`), which fills, keeps cash and
  triggers stops on daily bars, and refuses what the paper account refuses;
  and the market-data feed (`shadow/market.py`), which is the live class
  with only its history call replaced so that it can never see a later bar.

Two small, behaviour-preserving seams in `app/` make this possible: the
engine and the manager accept an `audit_logger`, and the manager reads a
record that can hand over one ticker's lines. Each fund keeps its own
record, in memory. The live audit log is the live position manager's state,
so a single shadow line in it would reset a real position's profit ladder.
A tripwire fails the run if any line reaches it.

## The day

A cycle journalled on day D is acted on at the open of the next session —
the race's own entry rule. At that open, stops the price gapped through fill
at the open; the position manager runs; then the entries, in **watchlist
order**, the only dispatch order production uses. During the session, stops
the low (or high, for a short) reaches fill at the stop. At the close,
dividends are paid or charged and the book is marked. Costs are 0.10% of
notional per side on every fill.

A line the model gave no answer on, or that the paper account held, is
skipped by every fund that day: the race's own rule. The names skipped
because the paper account held them are counted every cycle day
(`held_names` in `logs/funds.json`, and on the 4 Funds page): a name the
real account holds for weeks is out of every fund's reach for those weeks.

- **Shorts.** A fund may not short a name the paper account has been
  refused a short in (read from the live audit log). A refusal counts only
  from the day it happened, never backwards: a fund acting on a cycle
  journalled on day D is blocked only by refusals recorded on day D or
  earlier, both dated by their UTC day, the calendar the funds date cycles
  on (`not_shortable_since` in `logs/funds.json`). The calibration copy
  reads them the same way: the real account could not know a refusal before
  it happened either.
- **Price gaps.** On a day the price source has no bar for a ticker that
  traded, that ticker's position is left as it was and marked at its last
  close, the same for every fund. The share of missing ticker-days is
  reported for each calendar month (missing ticker-days ÷ watchlist tickers
  × sessions, from the fund test's first session; `price_gaps` in
  `logs/funds.json`, and on the 4 Funds page). Above 2% in the latest month
  (`MAX_PRICE_GAP_SHARE` in `config/settings.py`), the daily health check
  raises a warning that reaches the owner's phone with the brief.

Neither report is a fund result, so both are shown before calibration
passes.

## Calibration first

Before the funds count, a copy of the model fund starts from the paper
account's real positions and cash and follows the same signals for 15
trading days. Each day it shows the equity gap to the real account and every
trade that differs, with the reason. The real account's closing equity,
cash, positions, stops and fills are recorded by the heartbeat after every
run (`logs/account.jsonl`, read-only calls), because nothing else recorded
them. A read that failed is recorded as unknown, never as an empty book, and
such a line is never used as the starting point. Each part of a line says
when it was read, so a fill that landed between two reads is counted once.

The owner approved the pass rule on 24 Sep 2026: six conditions over 15
trading days, including a two-way trade match (at least 90% of the real
account's trades found in the sim, and at least 90% of the sim's trades
found in the real account, each within one session). If calibration fails,
the cause is fixed and logged in the pre-registration's Amendments table
(and in `shadow/schedule.py`'s `CALIBRATION_FIXES`), and the fixed
simulation is re-run on every calibration day so far, from the same starting
snapshot: every day must pass all the conditions again. At least 5
calibration days must come after the last fix, so calibration ends at day 15
or the last fix + 5 days, whichever is later; a re-run that fails on an
earlier day is a new fail, and a difference whose stated reason turns out to
be a bug counts as a fail (the owner's rule of 25 Sep 2026). The 4 Funds
page shows the days passed, the last fix and the days since it. The rule
takes effect with the calibration start date, once the first complete
account snapshot exists; that date lives in `shadow/schedule.py` and is set
by a reviewed change, never by the code; a test fails if it is set while the
rule is still unapproved, and an unapproved rule can never read "passed".

## The fund test's start, and the gate

The fund test's start is fixed (the owner's decision of 26 Sep 2026): the
funds act on the cycle journalled on **Monday 28 Sep 2026**, from the next
open, so the first fund session is **29 Sep 2026** and the sample runs from
it (`FUND_FIRST_CYCLE` and `FUND_START` in `shadow/schedule.py`). It does
not move when calibration is delayed; only the reading waits.

The fund test **counts only after calibration passes**. Until calibration's
verdict is "passed", no fund is run: nothing about a fund's book, equity or
trades is calculated or shown, in `logs/funds.json`, on the 4 Funds page or
in any log (`shadow.run.build`; a test proves no fund number appears). The
smoke check (`python -m shadow.smoke`) keeps to the same gate: until the
funds record says calibration passed, it runs only sessions before 29 Sep.
When calibration passes, every fund is run from 28 Sep with the code as
merged: after a calibration fix, with the fixed code only. A fix made during
calibration can only be motivated by a calibration difference, since no
fund result exists before it passes.

The fund test is read on the race's look days (estimated 22 Dec 2026,
22 Mar 2027 and 16 Jun 2027): 60, 120 and 180 fund sessions as planned. Its
bars come from an O'Brien-Fleming-type alpha-spending rule (Lan-DeMets,
`analysis.decision_gate.spending_bars`) at its own share of the planned 180
sessions: 3.40, 2.41 and 2.02 as planned (exactly 3.395, 2.407, 2.015). A
look that comes before calibration has passed is skipped: it is not read and
spends none of the 5%, and the next look's bar comes from the same rule with
the bars actually used before it. The race's own bars (3.47, 2.45, 2.00) are
unchanged. The 4 Funds page shows the plan beside calibration
(`calibration.fund_test_plan`), with any skipped look. That plan is a
forecast; what is read at each look is in
[the checkpoint verdict](#the-checkpoint-verdict).

### Late runs

The owner's decision of 25 Sep 2026, for calibration only: the race and the
funds do not change. When the real account's run falls outside market hours
(before 09:30 or after the 16:00 close in New York, 13:00 on a half day),
its broker refuses the entries ("market is closed") and its profit-ladder
pass cannot run. The calibration copy acts at the next open whatever time
the run was, so it mirrors the real account instead: it does not make an
entry the real broker refused as "market is closed" outside hours, and it
skips a ladder pass the journal says could not run. A refusal during market
hours is never mirrored (that is a fault of the real account's clock, and
it stays a difference). The mirror goes line by line: if a later run the
same day traded the name, the refused line is still not made and the line
the real engine accepted is traded as usual. Each mirrored entry and pass
is counted and listed on the 4 Funds page (`calibration.mirrored` in
`logs/funds.json`), never hidden, and none is a difference. **If more than
2 calibration days need this, calibration stops**: late runs on that many
days are a schedule problem to fix, not something to copy around. A day is
counted the evening its cycle is journalled, without waiting for the copy
to reach it, so the warning normally comes with the next day's brief. A
stopped calibration gives neither a pass nor a fail and no end date until
the owner decides; the 4 Funds page shows it, and the daily health check
raises a warning that reaches the owner's phone with the brief.

## The checkpoint verdict

The layout the owner approved on 6 Oct 2026
(`docs/research/checkpoint-verdict-plan.md`), with the five open readings
answered "yes" (pre-registration, Amendment 2026-10-06). It changes no rule,
no bar and no arm: the fund test's decision is the race's own code
(`analysis.decision_gate.decide`) at the fund test's own bar, and the tables
are made by `analysis/verdict.py`.

- **Calibration is judged on the first night the look is readable** (owner
  reading 3), for both the fund test's skip and the race's "unavailable"
  after-tax record, so the two can never disagree. If it has not passed
  that night, the look is skipped: "Not read, no part of the 5% spent, no
  bar used. The next bars come from the same rule using only the looks
  actually read." The skip in `shadow/fund_test.py` (`skipped_look`) stays
  a forecast only.
- **Read** once the funds reach the look's last close and the look's
  after-tax record exists, everything cut at that close: the fund sessions
  since 29 Sep 2026; the bar by the spending rule of section 11.7 from the
  looks actually read (bars already used stay as they were); the six paired
  before-tax tests (Newey-West, lag 5); the coin flip, the model fund's
  total return against the 95th percentile of the 1,000 coin-flip funds'
  total returns (a tie fails); the after-tax record; and one downturn
  number, the race's VT figure (owner reading 4: "downturn not measured"
  means the owner decides). A by-hand run with fewer than 1,000 coin-flip
  funds makes no record and says why (`fund_test.note`). With 180 or more
  fund sessions before the final look, the owner decides.
- **Made once and frozen** (owner reading 2), like the after-tax record:
  `fund_test.looks` in `logs/funds.json` carries each look's record and its
  table unchanged every night after. It is made again only if a bug fix
  moves the look's window, the old one kept inside it (`superseded`).
- **The verdict.** `fund_test.verdict` is the first deciding look's; later
  looks are recorded for reading only. The document's last key, `verdict`,
  is the one combined line of section 11.8 (owner reading 1): "The answer is
  the index as soon as either test says 'no arm trades', or the two pick
  different arms; it is the arm when both pick the same one; otherwise 'no
  decision yet'. An early answer is final; section 9 only delays the money
  to June 2027." Before any look it is `{"text": "no decision yet"}`.
- **The bar alert** (owner Addition 1). When the bar used at a look differs
  from the planned bar (3.40, 2.41, 2.02), the table's title ends "Bar x
  (planned y): log it in the Amendments table.", and the owner's phone gets
  *⚠️ Fund test bar differs from the plan* on the night the record is made
  (sent by `scoring-prices` after the shadow funds run; see
  [Phone notifications](/phone)). A re-run of that job does not send it
  again. If that night's push fails (`main` cannot be fetched, or ntfy
  refuses it), it is not sent again; the look's table title still ends
  "Bar x (planned y): log it in the Amendments table." The race's bars are
  the fixed 3.47, 2.45 and 2.00 and cannot differ, so the race has no such
  alert.
- **On the 4 Funds page**, a "Checkpoint verdict" card near the top shows
  each test's deciding look's table (else its latest look's) and the
  combined line; before any look it is one card, "Checkpoint verdict: no
  decision yet". The fund-test banner counts fund sessions of the planned
  180 and names the fund test's own next bar, not the race's (owner
  reading 5).
- **Examples** (owner Addition 2): `python tests/verdict_examples.py` prints
  one example of each table from test data, every line marked TEST DATA and
  every date in 2099. Tests fail if an example could reach a real output:
  the real writers refuse a test-data table, the real table builders refuse
  any date outside 2026-09-23 to 2027-12-31, and nothing under `logs/` or in
  the pages may hold "TEST DATA" or "2099-".

## Three exploratory funds

Three more simulated funds use the model's own signals and the same
machinery, and differ from the model fund in one thing each. They start with
the others, pay the same costs and follow the same rules, and they cannot
change any decision: each only answers one question, against the model fund
(its total return beside the model fund's, and the mean daily difference
with its Newey-West t, lag 5).

- **Model, highest conviction first**: when the book cannot take every
  signal, it buys in order of conviction, highest first, instead of
  watchlist order. Does the buying order matter?
- **Model, sized by conviction**: each new position's size is the normal
  size times 0.25 (conviction 0.30-0.40), 0.5 (0.40-0.50), 1.0 (0.50-0.60)
  or 1.5 (0.60 and above). In this system the normal size is the
  per-ticker cap (5% of equity for a company, 12% for a broad fund, and so
  on); the ATR only sets the stop. The fund's own engine reads the cap
  scaled by the signal's conviction, for its own entries only; production
  code is not changed, and every portfolio limit (exposure groups, sleeve
  budget, gross exposure, stock-market, position count) still applies. Its
  maximum drawdown is shown beside the model fund's.
- **Model, entered the same day** (`model_same_day`, the owner's decision
  of 26 Sep 2026): each line is entered on its own day, as the paper account
  does, at the price recorded on the journal line when its signal was made
  (the line's `live` record, on every cycle line since 28 Sep 2026): a buy
  at the recorded ask, a short at the recorded bid, or at the recorded last
  trade when there is no quote, plus the same 0.10%. The entry is made after
  that session's stop checks for the positions already held, so a new
  position's stop is first checked at the next session's open; the position
  manager runs at the next open, as for the model fund. The engine sizes
  and stops it on the recorded price, with the ATR as known at that
  session's open (daily bars say nothing about the range before the moment
  the signal was made). A line with no usable recorded price, or recorded
  outside regular New York hours (09:30 to 16:00, 13:00 on a half day), is
  not entered, and each is counted by reason on the 4 Funds page. Does
  buying on the signal's own day change the result? Its first cycle is the
  one journalled on the first fund session: the 28 Sep cycle, which the
  others enter at the 29 Sep open, it would have entered on 28 Sep, before
  the sample. A test proves every other fund's output is byte-identical with
  and without it.

Production and the calibration copy keep watchlist order and the normal
size. Each fund's closed trades are also split into longs and shorts (the
number, the mean net return and the hit rate; "too few" under 20).

## Exploratory tests A, B and C, and results only at checkpoints

Pre-registration section 13 (2026-09-28), in `shadow/exploratory.py`:

| Fund | Rule | Compared with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `momentum_200` (A) | the momentum signal, kept only when the line's live price is on its side of the 200-day average of final closes before the signal's day | the momentum fund (and, in the race, the momentum arm) |
| `vt_timing` (B) | VT when its month-end close is above the average of its last 10 month-end closes, else BIL; switched at the next open, 0.10% a side, from the 2026-09-30 month-end | the VT fund |
| `momentum_pullback` (C) | the momentum signals, entered by a limit at the signal price minus (short: plus) half the line's ATR14, standing 3 sessions | the momentum fund |

**Between checkpoints, only counters** (`funds.json` → `exploratory.counters`):
the share of momentum's signals A's veto removes, B's switches and state,
C's fill rate, and the lines `model_same_day` could not enter. They are
counted from the journal and the prices, without running a fund.

**At a checkpoint** (the night the race first reaches a look, and only once
calibration has passed), the three exploratory funds and A, B and C are run
from the fund start. Their results, the race side (A's race arm, the insider
arm's test, C's filled against missed signals) and the table of
Benjamini-Hochberg adjusted p-values and Deflated Sharpe Ratios (N from
`docs/research/graveyard.md`) go into one record per look
(`exploratory.checkpoints`), carried unchanged every night after. None of it
can change the decision.

**Prepared, switched off until their dates** (pre-registration sections 13.11
and 13.12). The voting arm `model_vote` asks the model four more times on
each answered line, with the same archived input, and trades the side that
has at least 3 of the 5 votes. From 2026-12-22 it joins the checkpoint
record: its race arm under `race`, its fund under `tests` (against
`model_vote_comparator`, a fund that runs the model's own answers on exactly
the vote's lines, so the four funds are untouched), both in the
Benjamini-Hochberg table, and the descriptive IC comparison under `vote_ic`;
between checkpoints only its counters (`exploratory.counters.model_vote`:
lines voted, answered, dropped, not voted, not asked, answered lines with
no vote record, calls). The weekly thesis check is a log;
from 2027-04-01 its description (the forward returns of names it called
BROKEN against VALID) goes under `thesis_check` at the checkpoints, and its
counters under `exploratory.counters.thesis_check`. Before those dates none
of these keys exists.

## How often the buying order matters (report only)

Each cycle the engine works through the day's signals one at a time, in
watchlist order. When the book runs out of room part-way -- cash, the gross
cap, the sleeve budget, an exposure-group cap, the stock-market limit, the
position count -- the signals left are refused, and which ones got in was
decided by their place in the list. `shadow/order_matters.py` counts those
days for the real paper account (from its own audit log) and for every
simulated fund: the date, the signals skipped with their conviction and the
limit that was full, and whether a skipped signal had a higher conviction
than one that was bought. A refusal counts only when a portfolio limit bound
it; a ticker already at its own cap would have been refused in any order.
The running count is in `logs/funds.json` (`order_matters`) and on the
4 Funds page. It changes nothing: the order stays the watchlist order, in the
simulation and in production.

## Where to read it

The **4 Funds** page of the desk, and `logs/funds.json`, written nightly by
**Actions → shadow funds** together with the race's gate
(`logs/race_gate.json`). The page keeps to the numbers, the dates and the
verdicts; each of its cards links to the section of this page that explains it.

```bash
python -m shadow.run --processes 4        # the nightly job, JSON to stdout
python analysis/horse_race.py --gate-json # the race's gate, JSON to stdout
```
