What gets traded lives in `config/instruments.py`, version-controlled rather
than in an environment variable, so a change shows up in a diff — the same
reasoning that keeps the guardrail thresholds in code.

**Funds are the core holding and single names the satellite.** That ordering
is deliberate: a broad fund is diversified by construction, while a
stock-picking edge is unproven here, and the sleeve budgets decline to assume
one.

## Sleeve 2b — the goods themselves (10)

| Role | Tickers |
|---|---|
| Precious metals | GLD, SLV |
| Industrial metals | CPER |
| Energy commodities | USO, UNG |
| Agriculture | CORN, WEAT, SOYB, CANE, JO |

Alpaca trades `us_equity`, `us_option` and `crypto` only, so futures are out
of reach and physical metal is not something a brokerage account can hold.
These US-listed funds are the only route to the commodities rather than the
companies that mine and drill them.

<Warning>
  **A single-commodity fund is not a diversified fund, and is capped below
  even a single stock.** Three risks pile on that no equity carries:

  1. **Roll decay.** A futures-backed fund rolls contracts monthly. In
     contango it sells cheap and buys dear every roll, so it can bleed value
     while spot is flat. `USO` is the notorious case. These are not
     buy-and-hold instruments.
  2. **Issuer credit risk.** `JO` is an exchange-traded **note** — unsecured
     debt of its issuer, not a fund holding anything. If the issuer fails the
     note can go to zero with coffee trading normally. ETNs can also be called
     by the issuer.
  3. **Thinness.** Several trade a small fraction of a broad fund's volume, so
     the spread is a real cost and a stop can fill badly.
</Warning>

### Rice is not reachable

There is no US-listed fund tracking rice. Rough-rice futures exist, but no ETF
or ETN tracks them, and the broad agriculture baskets (`DBA`) hold grains,
softs and livestock without it. Coffee is reachable only through `JO`, with
the ETN caveat above. This is a limit of what trades on a US exchange, not a
choice.

<Note>
  These tickers were selected from knowledge and **have not been verified
  against live market data** — the sandbox that wrote them cannot reach Yahoo.
  Run the **verify tickers** workflow in the Actions tab before the first live
  cycle; it resolves every symbol and reports anything that does not exist or
  trades too thin.
</Note>

## Three kinds, three caps

| Kind | Cap | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Single name | 5% | One company, idiosyncratic: it can gap on a fraud or a failed trial. |
| Broad fund | 12% | Hundreds of positions. Its tail is truncated in a way a company's is not. |
| Single-commodity fund | 4% | "Fund" does no diversification work — coffee is one thing — plus roll decay, issuer risk and thin volume. |

The ordering is the point: **the tightest cap belongs to the commodity funds,
not the stocks.** Sizing a coffee ETN like `RSP` because both are technically
ETFs would be picking a cap by label rather than by risk.

Risk per trade scales roughly as `cap / volatility`. A first pass gave broad
funds 20% and measured ~1.9× the per-trade risk of a single name — bigger, not
safer. 12% keeps it near parity.
`backtest/compare_sleeves.py` is what measures this.

## Three portfolio limits

Per-ticker caps bound one position. They say nothing about the shape of the
book, and that gap was real: nothing stopped the engine opening MSFT, NVDA,
TSM, ASML and GOOGL on one morning — five positions, one bet, every
per-ticker cap satisfied.

| Limit | Value | What it stops |
|---|---|---|
| Exposure group | 25% | A one-bet book. Spans both sleeves, so XOM + `USO` + `UNG` is one energy bet made three times. |
| Single-name sleeve | 15% | Stock picking growing past its budget. |
| Fund sleeve | 45% | — the core allocation. |
| Gross exposure | 60% | Leverage. Leaves a 40% cash floor at all times. |

All three run **before any market-data fetch**, so a full book costs no quote,
and the tightest of them is passed into sizing as a ceiling — a partly-full
book sizes the next position *down* rather than placing it and rejecting it
after. A rejection names the limit that actually bound, so "no room under the
`'Energy'` group limit" is never reported as a gross-exposure problem.

The sleeve budgets sum to the gross cap, so gross binds only when a sleeve is
under-used rather than being a fourth independent limit.

### The shape this produces

A greedily-filled book, 100k account:

```
RSP 12%, IWM 12%, VGK 12%, EWJ 9%, MSFT 5%, LLY 5%, JPM 5%
funds 45% | names 15% | deployed 60% | cash 40%
funds are 75% of the portfolio
```

## The instrument kind is a privilege boundary

The caps differ by kind, which makes the classification something a model must
never control. If a signal could assert *"this is a broad fund"*, it could
enlarge its own position by claiming one.

- The kind resolves from `config/instruments.py`, keyed on the ticker.
- `LLMSignal` has no field that could carry one, and `extra="forbid"` turns an
  invented one into a validation error before any execution logic runs.
- An unrecognised ticker resolves to `EQUITY` — never the largest cap — so a
  misspelling can only size down. It also lands in its own exposure group, so
  it cannot consume another group's room.
- CI invariants enforce the schema stays closed, the fund caps are readable
  only where defined and applied, and all three portfolio limits reach the
  execution path.

## What the funds give up

Two of the five dimensions are **structurally absent** for any fund: nobody
publishes a price target on an index or on copper, and neither has insiders
filing Form 4. That is carried through rather than papered over —
`orchestrator/context.py` omits those sections instead of rendering them
"unavailable this cycle", and a separate `ETF_SYSTEM_PROMPT` asks a macro
question and sets a higher bar for a non-neutral call.

<Warning>
  **That higher bar interacts with the fund-heavy budget, and the interaction
  is untested.** The fund prompt defaults to NEUTRAL because a directional
  call on a broad index is macro timing — the hardest call in markets. With
  funds holding 75% of the
  budget, a prompt that rarely takes a side may leave the account mostly in
  cash. Whether that is prudence or paralysis is a question for the journal,
  not for this page.
</Warning>

## Overriding it

```bash
WATCHLIST=GLD,RSP,MSFT python -m orchestrator.heartbeat --once
```

<Note>
  An override changes *what is evaluated*, never how it is sized. A ticker
  reached this way is still classified by `config/instruments.py`, so anything
  unrecognised gets the single-name cap and its own exposure group.
</Note>
