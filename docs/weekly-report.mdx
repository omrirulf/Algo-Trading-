Three reports watch the experiment without touching it (the owner's request
of 27 Sep 2026). None of them trades, changes a rule or feeds anything that
decides: they are for reading, and one of them can ask the owner to look.

| Report | When | Made by | Record |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Drift | every run; judged weekly | `analysis/drift.py`, in the heartbeat | `logs/drift.json` |
| Risk digest | the week's first cycle day | `orchestrator/digest.py`, in the heartbeat | `logs/digest/<week>.json` |
| Concentration | the first night after a month ends | `analysis/concentration.py`, in the funds workflow | `logs/concentration.json` |

The week's first cycle day also writes `logs/weekly/<week>.md`, the three
joined (`analysis/weekly.py`), and sends it to the phone as its own
notification after the daily brief, with a link to that file.

## Drift

Each week, from the journal alone:

- the share of the model's answers that are NEUTRAL;
- the conviction of the answers that take a side: the mean, the share at or
  above the floor, and the count in each conviction group;
- agreement with the momentum rule, on the lines where both take a side;
- the long/short mix;
- failed calls by type (setup errors and model errors, as
  `analysis/call_errors.py` splits them), as shares of the calls asked;
- when each day's run started (minutes after 14:40 UTC) and which starter
  started it;
- the model, the host that served it, the reasoning level, screening, and
  the prompt fingerprint.

Held lines and lines that failed before the model was asked are left out:
the question is what the model did with the calls it got.

**The rule (the owner's, changed on 27 Sep 2026 before any data existed).**

- **Settings** (the model, the host, the reasoning level, screening, the
  prompt fingerprint): any change alerts **the same day**. Today's lines are
  compared with the last cycle day before; two values on one day also alert.
- **Numbers** (NEUTRAL share, mean conviction, agreement with momentum, long
  share, setup errors, model errors, lateness): the band is the mean plus or
  minus 2 sample standard deviations of **every** complete week before, from
  28 Sep 2026. No band until 4 weeks have the number. An alert fires only
  when a number is outside its band **2 weeks in a row**, each week against
  its own band. It is said once, on the first cycle day of the following
  week. The share at or above the floor and the share of days the Supabase
  starter started are shown with no band (the starter already has its own
  daily warning).

Weeks run Monday to Sunday (UTC). The first number alert is possible for
the week of 2 Nov 2026 (4 weeks of band, then 2 weeks outside), said on
9 Nov 2026 at the earliest.

<Note>
Expected false alerts, if nothing really changes (simulated with normal
weekly numbers, 37 weeks to June 2027): about 1 week in 165 for each number,
about 1.4 alerts in all over the 7 numbers by June 2027. A number that has
been exactly the same every week (0 setup errors, say) has a band of zero
width, so any change for 2 weeks in a row alerts. The price of the 2-week
rule: a real lasting change of 2 standard deviations is caught within 4
weeks only about a third of the time, because the first week outside widens
the band; a change of 3 standard deviations about 70% of the time.
</Note>

### The prompt fingerprint

Every cycle line carries `model_setup`: the model, the host it was asked at
(never a key or a path) and 12 hex characters of the SHA-256 of every fixed
text the model is sent -- the company prompt, the fund prompt's parts, the
user prompt's closing words, the answer schema, and the two sentences the
client adds on its own (`orchestrator.heartbeat.prompt_fingerprint`). It
changes when any of those texts changes. It does not cover the day's context
or the code that renders it.

## Risk digest

Once a week, for the names the paper account holds (its last snapshot):

- **earnings dates** in the next 14 days, listed by code from the date the
  cycle already reads (yfinance's calendar), for companies;
- **fund closures or changes, index changes, earnings news and major news**,
  flagged by the model from the headlines the cycle already fetched for each
  held name that week (`context.sources` in the journal; no new search).

The model answers with a headline's id, a kind and a one-sentence note, and
nothing else. Code keeps a flag only if it names a headline the model was
given, copies the link, title and source from that headline, strips anything
like a link from the note, and counts what it dropped. Headlines are
untrusted text; the prompt tells the model to read them as data.

**Batches.** The headlines go to the model at most 40 to a call, a name's
headlines always in the same call, and every call at once. The first digest
(2026-W40, 29 Sep 2026) sent the whole week in one call: 274 headlines, about
19,500 tokens. gpt-oss-120b, writing about 43 tokens a second that day, had
not answered when the call and its one retry each ran out the 300-second
timeout, and the report said only `failed: LLMError`. A batch of 40 is about
the size of the prompt the same model answers for each ticker every cycle.
A batch that fails is named in the status with the error it raised (for
example `LLMError: ... The read operation timed out (gave up after 2
attempt(s))`), the heartbeat's log says the same, and the other batches'
flags are kept.

**Cost cap: $1 a week.** Before the calls, the worst case (every character a
token, a full answer for every batch, each asked twice for an off-schema
retry) is priced; if it would take the week's spend past $1, or the model
has no price, no call is made and the digest says so. At gpt-oss-120b's
price, W40's 274 headlines make 9 batches and price at $0.14 in the worst
case, and no full week (at most 12 batches) can price above $0.25; a real
digest costs a cent or two. The measured cost is in the record.

The heartbeat runs it with `python -m orchestrator.heartbeat --weekly-digest`,
which builds the full model's provider and nothing else of a cycle: no
dispatcher, no broker, no journal line. CI keeps `orchestrator/digest.py`
away from `app/`, orders and files, and lets only the heartbeat import it.

## Concentration (monthly, descriptive only)

For each month's last trading day:

- **the book's beta to VT**: each position's ordinary least-squares beta to
  VT over the year of daily final closes before it, times its signed market
  value, summed and divided by equity (longs and shorts also shown apart);
- **each exposure group against its cap** (25%; Duration 30%), counted as
  the risk engine counts them;
- **breadth**: the share of the watchlist whose close is above the average
  of its last 50 closes;
- **VT's realized volatility**: the standard deviation of its daily returns
  in the month, times the square root of 252.

The book is the paper account's last snapshot of the month
(`logs/account.jsonl`). Prices are the raw daily closes the race uses. The
funds workflow makes each month once and commits it; the next weekly report
shows it, under a heading that names the month.

The account has been recorded only since 25 Sep 2026, so no month before
September 2026 has a book: August's report says `book: not measured` and
names the day it looked for. September's, made on the first night of
October, is the first with one. A month whose book is missing says why:
nothing recorded yet, or recorded only since a later day.
