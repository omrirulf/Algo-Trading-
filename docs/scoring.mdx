---
title: "Scoring the Journal"
description: "Joining recorded signals to what the market actually did — and refusing to overclaim from it"
---

The [signal journal](/orchestrator/journal) records what the model saw and what
it concluded. `analysis/score_journal.py` is the other half: it joins each
recorded signal to the return that actually followed, and answers the questions
the journal was built to make answerable.

```bash
python analysis/score_journal.py
python analysis/score_journal.py --horizon 5 --entry next
python analysis/score_journal.py --json > scores.json
```

It is read-only in every direction — it opens the journal, fetches price
history, and prints. It holds no broker keys, has no order path, and cannot
write to the journal it reads. CI greps `analysis/` to keep that true.

## What it answers

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="Does conviction predict the outcome?" icon="gauge">
    A rank correlation between conviction and the *signed* return — the move in
    the direction the model called, so a BEARISH call on a falling stock is a
    win — plus a bucket table from the lowest conviction band to the highest.

    The question underneath: is the number the engine gates on actually
    informative, or is it decoration?
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Is the conviction floor filtering the right signals?" icon="filter">
    `MIN_CONVICTION` (0.60) is the gate that decides what gets traded. The
    report splits scored signals either side of it and reports the *edge* — the
    difference in mean signed return between what was acted on and what was
    filtered.

    A negative edge means the floor is discarding the better signals, and the
    report says so in those words. That is a guardrail failing quietly, and it
    is exactly the sort of thing nobody notices without measuring.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Which dimension actually carried information?" icon="layer-group">
    Each per-dimension score against the ticker's raw forward return. Scores are
    themselves directional, so this is a direct test of whether news,
    technicals, fundamentals or the analyst view had any predictive content —
    or whether three of them are expensive decoration on a news reader.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Does conviction fall when the dimensions disagree?" icon="scale-unbalanced">
    The system prompt demands it: *"conviction is earned when independent
    dimensions agree, and it must fall when they conflict."* This compares the
    model's own scores against the conviction it attached.

    It needs **no price data at all**, so unlike everything else on the page it
    is answerable from the first cycle onward.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Is conviction drifting upward?" icon="chart-line">
    First half against second half. A model that creeps toward always-confident
    turns the 0.60 floor into a no-op without anything visibly breaking.
  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Was the context actually there?" icon="heart-pulse">
    What share of cycles ran with a gap, grouped by which source was missing,
    plus the bias distribution. If most cycles ran degraded, every other number
    on the page describes a different system than the intended one — and the
    report says so rather than leaving you to notice.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

## Entry timing is the one thing that could make this lie

A scoring tool is only worth running if it cannot flatter the strategy. There
is exactly one way this one could, and most of `analysis/returns.py` exists to
prevent it: **entering at a price that was already known when the signal was
produced.**

A signal generated at 22:00 because of a stock's 6% move that day, "entered" at
that day's close, scores as a brilliant call. It is not a call at all.

So the entry bar is chosen from the signal's *timestamp*, not its date:

| `--entry` | Behaviour |
|---|---|
| `auto` (default) | Same-session close if the signal fired before the close **and** carried an exact UTC timestamp; the next session's close otherwise. |
| `next` | Always the next session's close. Never wrong, mildly pessimistic — it gives up the same-day move a news signal is trying to capture. |
| `same` | Always the signal date's close. Only honest if you know the heartbeat runs during the session. |

<Note>
  The cutoff is 20:00 UTC, which is 16:00 ET under EDT. Under EST the close is
  actually 21:00 UTC, so in winter a 20:30 signal is misclassified as
  after-hours and falls back to the next session. That is the deliberate
  direction to be wrong in: it costs a little realism and cannot manufacture a
  look-ahead.
</Note>

Journal lines written before `ts_utc` existed carry a timezone-naive timestamp.
Those are never trusted with a same-day entry under `auto`.

## It reports sample sizes, and refuses to overclaim

A rank correlation over nine signals is arithmetic, not evidence. Every figure
is printed with its `n`, and anything below 20 observations is marked
`too few`:

```
dimension         n     rank corr
news             14         +0.61 — too few
```

Rank correlation is used throughout rather than Pearson. Forward returns have
fat tails, and with a few weeks of signals one earnings-day move would
otherwise set the sign of the whole answer.

Two weeks in, the honest output is a coverage table and a row of `too few`.
That is a useful thing to see, not a bug to work around.

<Warning>
  **This measures the signal, not the strategy's P&L.** Returns are
  close-to-close over the horizon and ignore the stop-loss, slippage and
  commission. A signal that was right about direction can still lose money
  after being stopped out on the way there.
</Warning>

## What it deliberately does not do

- **NEUTRAL signals are not scored.** A refusal to call a direction has no
  direction to be right or wrong about; folding them in as zeroes would drag
  every average toward the middle.
- **Signals too recent to have an outcome are `pending`, not flat.** Scoring
  them as 0% would quietly bias every average toward zero as the journal grows.
- **A ticker whose history cannot be fetched is reported, not dropped.** Silent
  exclusion is how a sample becomes unrepresentative without anyone noticing.

Each of those shows up as a row in the coverage table, so the gap between
"signals produced" and "signals scored" is always visible.

## Machine-readable output

`--json` emits the same figures for a notebook or a dashboard, including
`min_sample_for_confidence` so a downstream consumer can apply the same
threshold rather than inventing its own.
