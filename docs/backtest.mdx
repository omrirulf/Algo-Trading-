title: "Risk-engine backtest"
description: "What a 2× ATR stop and a 5% cap actually do, without waiting for live trades"
---

The model's judgement cannot be reconstructed from price history, so the
*strategy* cannot be backtested. The deterministic half can: the ATR stop and
the position cap are pure functions of price and volatility, and two years of
real bars answer questions that otherwise need months of live trading.

```bash
python backtest/run_backtest.py AAPL
python backtest/run_backtest.py AAPL --csv bars.csv    # offline, reproducible
```

## What it tells you

```
STOP MULTIPLIER  (currently ATR_STOP_MULTIPLIER = 2)
setting          n   stopped   gapped  risk/trade  median ret    worst
1x ATR          98     73.5%     2.8%       0.14%      -2.54%   -0.18%
2x ATR          98     41.8%     4.9%       0.27%      -2.46%   -0.32%  <- current
4x ATR          98     10.2%     0.0%       0.54%      -2.20%   -0.56%
```

Two things worth knowing before you tune anything:

**A 5% position cap does not put 5% at risk.** What you actually risk is
quantity × stop distance. At a 2× ATR stop that is around **0.27%** of equity
per trade — twenty times smaller than the cap suggests. `risk/trade` is the
number the two settings jointly decide, and the one worth reasoning about.

**A tighter stop is not a safer one.** At 1× ATR nearly three quarters of
trades stop out, which is noise rather than protection, and the share that
*gaps through* the stop — filling worse than it — rises as the stop tightens.
That is the part a stop cannot protect you from at all.

## Three things that decide whether the output means anything

Each is a way a backtest quietly flatters itself, and each is pinned by a test
that was verified to fail when the guard is removed.

<AccordionGroup>
  <Accordion title="No look-ahead">
    ATR comes from bars up to and including the signal bar; entry is the
    *next* bar's open. A stop sized with volatility the trader could not yet
    have seen would understate stop-outs.

    Tested by running the same signal against two futures — one calm, one with
    a volatility explosion — and asserting the ATR and stop are identical.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Stops are intraday, not at the close">
    A stop is hit when the bar's **Low** crosses it. Checking closes would
    understate stop-outs dramatically, because most stops are hit and
    recovered within a session.

    Tested with a bar that dips far below the stop and closes above it.
  </Accordion>
  <Accordion title="Gaps fill worse than the stop">
    If a bar opens through the stop, the fill is the **open**, not the stop
    price. Assuming you always get your stop is the most common way a backtest
    invents money it would never have had.

    Tested with an overnight gap far below the stop, asserting the fill is the
    open and is worse than the stop.
  </Accordion>
</AccordionGroup>

<Note>
  The stop and size come from `app.risk_engine` itself, not a reimplementation.
  Measuring a copy would measure the wrong code — and a copy would be free to
  drift from the thing that actually trades.
</Note>

## What it does not measure

<Warning>
  **There is no signal here.** Trades are opened at fixed intervals with no
  view attached, so the returns column describes the price series, not an edge.
  A multiplier that looks best by median return is telling you about those
  particular bars.

  Read `stopped`, `gapped` and `risk/trade` — those are mechanical and do not
  depend on signal quality. For whether the *signals* are any good, the only
  honest answer is [Scoring](/scoring) against realised returns.
</Warning>

Also not modelled: slippage, commission, and the interaction between positions
that `MAX_OPEN_POSITIONS` governs. Rows with fewer than 20 trades are marked
`(thin)`, the same threshold the signal scorer uses.

## Options

| Flag | Effect |
|---|---|
| `--csv FILE` | Read bars from disk instead of yfinance — reproducible and offline. |
| `--period` | yfinance period (default `2y`). |
| `--side` | `buy` or `sell`. |
| `--horizon N` | Days held if never stopped (default 10). |
| `--every N` | Open a trade every N bars (default 5), so trades overlap less. |
| `--json` | Machine-readable. |

<Note>
  Overlapping trades share the same price moves, which would make any hit rate
  look more precise than it is. `--every` is what keeps the samples closer to
  independent.
</Note>
