title: "The Horse Race"
description: "The model, a coded rule and a coin flip, on the same lines against the same realised returns, after costs"
---

Every signal this system has ever traded on came from a model. The question
underneath the whole cost conversation — and underneath whether to keep a
model in the daily loop at all — is whether the model's judgement beats a
fixed rule reading only the price. `analysis/horse_race.py` is the thing that
asks it, and `rules/` is the rule.

```bash
python analysis/horse_race.py
python analysis/horse_race.py --horizon 5 --floor 0.5 --cost-per-side 0.0015
python analysis/horse_race.py --per-trade      # list every scored trade
```

It runs on its own, credential-free, every trading night after the close
(**Actions → horse race**), and on any pull request that touches an arm. It
reads the journal, fetches prices, and prints. It cannot trade and it cannot
move a parameter. What it is allowed to decide, and when, is written down in
the [pre-registration](/horse-race-preregistration) before the numbers.

## The decision gate comes first

Every run opens with the pre-registered gate, applied by
`analysis/decision_gate.py` rather than by whoever reads the output:

```
DECISION GATE (pre-registered; applied by this code, not by a reader)
independent days since 2026-09-23: 0 of 60 (= 180 trading days) — NO DECISION YET
Next checkpoint: 20 independent days (= 60 trading days), estimated 2026-12-22, bar t > 3.47
```

- **Only lines journalled on or after 2026-09-23 count**, the day the model
  arm changed. The race runs everything on that window first, then prints
  the whole journal after it, marked for reading only.
- **Independent days** are complete blocks of 3 scored entry days.
- **Three planned looks**, at 20, 40 and 60 independent days, with
  O'Brien-Fleming bars of t > 3.47, 2.45 and 2.00. A look is computed on its
  own first entry days only, so it says the same thing every night after it
  is reached. Nothing is decided between looks.
- **Index first.** The arm the rule picks must also beat VT, bought at the
  same opens and held over the same sessions, at the same bar — or no arm
  trades and the answer is to hold the index. A VT day the price source has
  not printed makes the look wait; once five later VT sessions are out and
  that day still is not, it is left out of the index test and counted.
- **Model watch.** Trigger (a), a one-time check on the zero-shorts replay,
  tripped and was closed on 25 Sep 2026: gpt-oss shorted 1 of the 31 lines
  where Opus shorted, and the owner kept gpt-oss, so the model arm is
  effectively long-only. Trigger (b), five answered days with no SHORT while
  SPY fell, was retired on 26 Sep 2026 by the owner's decision: with a
  long-only model it would only say that SPY fell. The owner's triggers (c)
  and (d) are printed on every run. (c): model errors above 5% of the calls
  that reached the model over five cycle days, counted from 25 Sep 2026;
  setup errors (our key or configuration) are shown but not counted. (d):
  the real paper account's equity more than 8% below its highest close since
  23 Sep 2026, or its return since the 23 Sep close more than 5 percentage
  points behind VT's (VT final closes only; a day with no final VT close is
  not judged on that part). The race reads the account from
  `logs/account.jsonl` and prints the current drawdown from the peak and
  the gap to VT in points even when nothing has tripped; with no usable
  record it says "not judged: no account record". (d) trips only on
  closing equity, after the 16:00 New York close. A latest reading taken
  during the session is printed on its own "mid-day" line, and when it is
  below the 8% line or more than 5 points behind (against VT's last final
  close) the race adds a separate warning marked mid-day that does not trip
  (d); the day is judged at its close. (d) is in `logs/race_gate.json`
  under `watch.d` (the mid-day reading as `midday` and `midday_warning`),
  and when that says it tripped the daily health check raises a warning
  that the brief sends to the owner's phone; a mid-day warning raises
  none. A trip means stop and tell the owner; nothing reverts or trades,
  and nothing that decides the race reads it. A run with more than 20% of
  its calls failed, of either kind, pages the owner's phone the same day.
- It also prints what the model calls cost over the window, and warns if any
  line in it was made with the screen on or at another reasoning level.

A run with any setting other than the registered ones (horizon, cost,
floor, seeds, sizing) says **SENSITIVITY RUN** and evaluates no look.

## Exploratory reports: conviction groups, longs and shorts

From 23 Sep 2026 the race also prints the trades split two ways, report
only: the model's, momentum's and the hybrid's by the conviction of the line
that opened them (0.30-0.40, 0.40-0.50, 0.50-0.60, 0.60 and above; the coin
flip's conviction is one fixed number, so it is not split), and every arm's
into longs and shorts. Each group
shows the number of trades, the mean net return and the hit rate, and reads
"too few" until it has 20 trades. They answer "does higher conviction mean
better trades?" and "do the longs and the shorts differ?"; they decide
nothing. The same numbers are in `logs/race_gate.json` and on the 4 Funds
page.

## Run timing and late runs

GitHub delivered the heartbeat's scheduled runs hours late on every day so
far, and nothing in the journal said so. Every run of the race now prints,
after the checks and before the tables, when each cycle day actually ran —
in the decision window and before it, labelled. `python
analysis/run_timing.py` prints the same table on its own.

```
RUN TIMING (when each cycle ran; report only, decides nothing)
day         part    New York     phase        vs sched  by        source
2026-09-17  before  11:49-15:23  session       +44 min  unknown   inferred  LATE
2026-09-22  before  11:07-11:45  session      +152 min  unknown   inferred  LATE
2026-09-23  window  11:06-12:20  session      +151 min  unknown   inferred  LATE
late-run days: 8 of 8; lines before the open: 0; after the close: 0
```

- **One line per cycle day** (the UTC date of its lines): the first and last
  journal line in New York time; where they fell (`pre_open`, `session`,
  `after_close`, or `no_session` on a weekend or holiday; half days are not
  known); minutes after the day's scheduled start; what started the run; and
  **LATE** when that was more than 30 minutes.
- **Where the timing comes from.** From the first heartbeat cycle after the
  change that added it (28 Sep 2026 at the earliest; 25 Sep's cycle ran the
  code before it), every journal line the heartbeat's cycle step writes
  carries a run block (`run`: what started it — `schedule`, `scheduler`,
  `backup` or `manual`, where `manual` is any dispatch, a person's or a
  Claude Routine's, and `scheduler` the Supabase starter — the scheduled
  start, when the cycle step started, minutes late, and the GitHub run id;
  from 26 Sep 2026 also `source`, the starter that asked for the run), and
  a day that has one is read from it. The row ends `via <source>` when the
  block names one: `supabase-cron` is the main starter, anything else a
  backup.
  Earlier days are inferred: the first line against the heartbeat's cron
  history, written down as `SCHEDULE_HISTORY` in `analysis/run_timing.py`
  (13:05 UTC on 14 Sep, 15:05 on 15–18 Sep, 12:35 from 21 Sep until the
  change), and "started by" reads `unknown`. The 14:40 UTC start has no row
  there: every day under it carries its own scheduled start in the block.
- **What it showed.** Every cycle day from 15 to 25 Sep was late, by 44 to
  154 minutes, and every line was written during the session. None ran
  before the open: the 12:35 UTC pre-market slot never once arrived on time,
  and from 21 Sep a backup dispatch started each day at about 15:05 UTC. Once
  the change is merged the run starts at 14:40 UTC, after the open in both
  seasons.
- **A late run cannot move an entry price.** Every arm, the coin flip and VT
  enter at the open of the next session after the signal's day, so a late
  run changes when a signal is made, never the price it is entered at.
  `tests/test_race_entry_timing.py` pins it for a line in the session, after
  the close, after midnight UTC, before the open, on a Friday, before a
  holiday and on a day the price source never printed. Two of those can wait
  up to one session longer than strictly needed — a line before the open
  enters at the next day's open, and a line after midnight UTC (20:00 New
  York in summer, 19:00 in winter) carries the next UTC date — and neither
  has ever been written.
- The same rows are in `logs/race_gate.json` under `run_timing`. Report
  only: no look, bar or verdict reads them.

## The arms

| Arm | Role | What it is | Where it comes from |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **model** | incumbent | The full model's answer, exactly as journalled | `logs/journal/` |
| **momentum** | main comparator | Time-series momentum with the textbook parameters | `rules/momentum.py`, recomputed from the line's own technicals |
| **hybrid** | second main comparator | Momentum, with the line's own news score allowed one veto | `rules/hybrid.py`, recomputed from the technicals and the journalled `news_score` |
| **random** | floor | A coin flip, seeded from the ticker and the day | `rules/control.py`, recomputed the same way |
| **insiders** | exploratory | Cluster buying by officers and directors | `rules/insider_buying.py`, recomputed from the journalled INSIDER ACTIVITY section |

The main tables race the model against the two main comparators and the
floor. The exploratory arm is raced apart, on its own lines only, and can
never change the main decision — see [Exploratory arms](#exploratory-arms).

Each arm is put through the identical conviction floor, the identical stop and
sizing arithmetic (`backtest.simulate.simulate_trade`, the code that actually
trades), the identical round-trip cost, and scored against the identical price
history. The only thing that differs between the rows is the judgement.

## What the numbers mean

These definitions are printed at the top of every run, so a table can never be
read without them.

<Note>
**Net return** is a trade's signed return through the real ATR stop and
sizing, gap fills included, minus a fixed round-trip cost — `0.10%` per side,
`0.20%` a round trip, by default (`--cost-per-side`). The cost is charged to
every trade of every arm alike. It is a pre-registered assumption, not a
measurement of this broker.

**A hit** is a trade whose net return is strictly greater than zero. A gross
gain smaller than the round trip is a miss. A return of exactly zero is a miss.
There is no other threshold.

**A daily return** is the equal-weighted mean net return of the trades an arm
opened on one entry day. A day it opened none is `0` — it sat in cash.

**Final closes only.** Every price source is cut to the last session whose bar
was final at the moment the race ran (`analysis.returns.last_final_session`:
after 16:30 New York, today; before that, the previous session). A trade whose
exit bar is still being traded is *pending*, never scored on an intraday
print. Two runs on the same journal therefore agree on every resolved trade.
This is the fix for the day the same 46 trades scored a 37.0% hit rate at
14:27 New York and 32.6% at 17:05: the earlier run's 21 September "close" was
a price from the middle of the afternoon.
</Note>

## Why the daily table comes first

The per-trade table has an `n` column that overstates what it knows. Twenty
trades opened on the same Tuesday all held for the same three sessions of the
same market; they are one observation about that Tuesday, not twenty about
the arm. So the race's primary series is daily: one equal-weighted number per
arm per entry day, over the calendar of every entry day any arm traded.

The primary test is the **paired difference, model minus momentum, day by
day**, and beside it the same difference, model minus hybrid. Each mean is
reported with a Newey-West standard error at `lag = horizon`, which is the
overlap a three-session hold creates between consecutive days. As a check,
the same difference is reported over every third entry day only — trades
that cannot overlap at all — with a plain standard error.

```
# illustrative numbers; the real ones are in the latest run's summary
PRIMARY: daily equal-weighted net return, sorted by mean per day (5 entry days)
arm         days traded  mean/day  t (NW)  cumulative
random         5      5    -0.12%   -1.10      -0.60%
momentum       5      5    -0.55%   -2.31      -2.72%
model          5      2    -0.35%   -1.02      -1.74%
buy & hold                                     +0.70%  watchlist (80/80 priced)
SPY                                            +2.10%

Paired difference, model minus momentum, day by day
entry day     model  momentum     diff
2026-09-16   -0.41%    -0.92%   +0.51%
...
mean diff +0.20% | t (Newey-West, lag 3) 0.61 | n=5 days
non-overlapping, every 3rd entry day: mean diff +0.12% | t 0.40 | n=2 days
```

The rows are sorted by mean net return, and the cumulative column compounds
the daily series so it can sit beside buy-and-hold of the watchlist and of SPY
over the same days.

## Funds and companies, separately

About four in five names on the watchlist are funds. A fund has no analysts,
no insiders and no earnings, so a model that reads all of those may behave
differently on the two halves, and one table over both would hide it. Every
table is printed three times: all names, funds only, companies only. The
buy-and-hold row of each split is that split's own basket.

## The coin flip, a thousand times

One coin flip per line is one draw. The race draws a thousand (`--seeds`),
keeping the journalled flip as seed 0, and reports the 5th–95th percentile
band of mean net return, hit rate and mean daily return — and where each arm
sits inside that band, as a percentile.

The band is drawn on **each arm's own lines**, not on every line. The null for
"did the model read anything" is not "a coin flip on the whole watchlist"; it
is "a coin flip on the lines the model chose to trade". Selection stays the
arm's; only the direction is replaced. An arm at the 60th percentile of its own
band did what chance does on those lines.

The band also answers a question the first race left open: the coin flip
scored a 45.7% hit rate, not 50%. Nothing in the definition penalises it — a
hit was, and is, simply a net return above zero — but two mechanical things
push a coin flip below half. The stop is asymmetric: a stopped trade is a loss
whichever way the coin fell, and 7% of the coin's trades were stopped. And the
round trip is now charged, so a coin that lands on a name that barely moved is
a miss on both sides. The rest is the width of the band at that `n`: with a
few hundred overlapping trades, 45.7% is inside the 5th–95th range chance
draws for itself.

## Who gets asked

Every arm is offered exactly the same lines. Two kinds are offered to no arm:
a ticker already in the book, where the model was never asked and a rule
holding the same book would not have been either; and, since 24 Sep 2026, a
line where the model gave no answer at all (a timeout or a failed call). That
second one is a bug fix in the pre-registration's Amendments: the rules used
to trade such a line while the model sat in cash, which compared the arms on
different lines and charged the model for an outage.

The coverage table reconciles every line so nothing goes missing. An older
run, from before that fix, looked like this:

```
COVERAGE AND RECONCILIATION
journal lines 552 | unparseable 0 | held, offered to no arm 80 | no timestamp 0 | offered to every arm 472
arm        offered neutral a side < floor pending resolved dropped scored
model          456     385     71       0      23       48       2     46
momentum       472      73    399     278       0      121       3    118
random         472       0    472       0     211      261       3    258
```

`offered = neutral + a side`; `a side = below floor + pending + resolved`;
`resolved = dropped + scored`, and the run says in words whether every
resolved line is scored or named as dropped. Before 2026-09-23 a cheap screen
answered NEUTRAL on the names it did not escalate; those lines are the
model's NEUTRAL, because the funnel was the design then. The screen has been
off since 2026-09-23, and the pre-registration pins it off.

## What the rule reads, and why that is the point

The momentum arm reads one thing: the `TechnicalSnapshot` the cycle already
computed from price history and already showed the model in its prompt. No
news, no fundamentals, no analysts, no insiders. That is not a limitation of
the rule; it is the experiment. The model sees all of those too. If the model
does not beat a rule that sees strictly less, then reading the rest bought
nothing.

The rule itself, from Moskowitz, Ooi and Pedersen (*Journal of Financial
Economics*, 2012) and its short-horizon form in Medhat and Schmeling (*Review
of Financial Studies*, 2022):

- **BULLISH** when the 63-bar return is positive *and* price is above its
  50-day average; **BEARISH** when both are negative; **NEUTRAL** when they
  disagree.
- **Conviction** is the quarter's return in units of volatility: since
  2·√(63/252) = 1, that is simply `|return_63d| / annualised_volatility`,
  clamped to [0, 1].

<Warning>
  **Nothing in the rule is fitted to this journal, and nothing will be.** 63
  bars is a quarter. 50 is the confirmation average the literature uses. Two
  sigmas is the choice that leaves no leftover constant. The race judges the
  rule rather than tuning it, and a good number in the race is not a reason to
  start. The cost per side and the horizon are fixed the same way, before the
  numbers, in the pre-registration.
</Warning>

## The hybrid: momentum that listens to the news

The momentum arm reads strictly less than the model. The hybrid reads one
thing more: the `news_score` the model call on that line already produced,
a number in [-1, 1] for how the headlines read. It is on every line the
journal has a signal for, so the arm is recomputable over the whole history
and makes **no new model call**. The score comes from the same call as the
model arm's answer, so choosing the hybrid would still need a daily
news-scoring call; see the pre-registration's "The hybrid is not model-free".

The rule: momentum decides the direction; the score can only stand the arm
aside. When the score points against the trend the hybrid is NEUTRAL; when it
does not, the hybrid takes momentum's side with conviction equal to the mean
of momentum's conviction and the score's magnitude. A score of exactly zero is
"no view" and does not veto. Equal weights is the only weighting that involves
no fitting, and a sign test has no parameter to tune.

The question it asks is the second one the pre-registration decides on: is
the full model's judgement worth more than a trend rule that merely listens
to the news?

## Exploratory arms

`rules/insider_buying.py` is BULLISH when, in the 180 days before the line,
two or more distinct insiders made open-market purchases and the open-market
shares bought exceed the shares sold; otherwise NEUTRAL, never BEARISH,
because selling carries almost no information. The window is the six-month
aggregation of Lakonishok and Lee (2001) and is also the window the prompt's
own snapshot carries. The parsing in `orchestrator/insiders.py` already
excludes stock awards, option exercises, gifts and tax withholding, with
tests; the arm further excludes the issuer buying back its own shares and
ten-per-cent holders, because the thesis is about people.

Four in five names are funds, which have no insiders, and a cluster is rare
on the rest: on the first week's journal the arm took a side on 8 of 539
lines, all one company. So it is raced apart:

- **on its own lines only**, paired against the model and the momentum rule
  on exactly those tickers on exactly those days;
- **at two horizons**, the race's 3 sessions and 20 sessions, because the
  effect it is named after plays out over months;
- marked **too few** until it has 20 scored trades on 20 distinct entry days;
- and it **cannot change the main decision**, whatever it shows. An
  exploratory arm is a hypothesis for the next registration, not evidence in
  this one.

## The agreement, split

On the first week's journal the model and the momentum rule agreed on 100% of
the 60 lines where both took a side. The race now splits that number into
longs and shorts, day by day, and says in words whether the agreement was
automatic. It was not one-sided: 23 of the 60 agreements were long and 37
short, with shorts dominating on 16 September and longs on 17 September. What
the split does show is that the model has not yet once called against the
trend. Whatever it adds is selectivity — which lines to trade — not direction.

## The arms are on every journal line

Every line the heartbeat writes carries an `arms` field beside the model's
signal — on screened lines, held lines and failed lines too, computed inside
`journal.record` itself so no call site can leave it off. Nothing in the cycle
reads it back. The signal handed to the engine is the model's, untouched, and
CI pins that: `rules/` cannot reach an order path, write a file or read a
credential.

The harness does not trust that field. It recomputes both rule arms from the
`technicals` on each line, which is what lets it race the model over every
line ever journalled, and is why the coin flip is seeded from the day rather
than a clock.

## Limits

The per-trade rows are not a portfolio: no overlapping positions, no cash
constraint, no compounding. The daily rows equal-weight each day's trades and
compound the days, which is a portfolio only if it rebalances to equal weight
every morning — a stated assumption, not a claim about the live account.

Anything marked *too few* is arithmetic, not evidence. A few weeks inside one
market regime are fewer independent observations than any `n` here suggests,
which is why the pre-registration counts entry days rather than trades, and
why the race runs nightly rather than when someone remembers to ask.
