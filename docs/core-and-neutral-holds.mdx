---
title: "The core and neutral holds"
description: "Why the account stays invested when the model has no opinion, and what holds the idle money"
---

The signal book only invests where the model takes a side, and on most days
it takes a side on few names. On 24 Nov 2026 that left the paper account
**45% invested** with the other 55% in cash that nobody had chosen to hold:

- over the eight weeks before, the engine refused 2,191 signals as `NEUTRAL`,
  so the fund sleeve — 70% of the account — sat mostly empty;
- the single-name sleeve, 25%, was refusing signals for lack of room — 132
  times in the same weeks.

`MAX_GROSS_EXPOSURE_PCT` was already 95%, and no cap was the reason. The
reason was that "no opinion" meant "hold cash". Two changes give it a
different meaning.

<Warning>
  **Only the core is switched on.** Neutral holds are built and tested but
  `NEUTRAL_HOLD_ENABLED` is `False`: the core went live first and alone, so
  that its first cycles can be read on their own. Until that switch is
  turned on, `NEUTRAL` means "no trade" for every ticker, and section 1
  below describes what will happen, not what does.
</Warning>

## 1. NEUTRAL on a broad fund means "hold the normal weight"

| The model says | On a broad fund | On anything else |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `BULLISH` | long, up to the full 12% cap | long, up to its cap |
| `NEUTRAL` | **long, up to 6%** — half the cap | no trade |
| `BEARISH` | short, up to the cap | short, up to its cap |

A model with no view on Brazil should hold no Brazil. A model with no view on
the US stock market or on Treasuries has said nothing against owning them. So
a `NEUTRAL` on a `BROAD_FUND` opens a long at
`NEUTRAL_HOLD_FRACTION` (0.5) of that fund's own cap. Focused funds,
commodity funds and single names keep the old meaning.

A neutral hold is an ordinary entry in every other respect: a bracket with an
ATR stop, every portfolio limit, the [profit ladder](/position-management)
afterwards. Four rules keep it the lowest-priority entry in the book:

- **It only opens.** It never adds to or trims a position already held.
- **It stops at its share of the fund sleeve.** A neutral hold is opened only
  while the fund sleeve stands below `NEUTRAL_HOLD_SLEEVE_PCT` (50%), so the
  last 20% of the fund budget is kept for funds the model has a view on.
- **It is delivered last.** The cycle sets every neutral hold aside and
  dispatches it after all of the day's directional signals.
- **It counts the orders already sent.** The broker's position list lags its
  own fills by a few seconds, so a neutral hold is sized against the list
  *plus* the orders the engine accepted earlier in the same pass -- the same
  memory every signal is sized against since 25 Nov 2026
  (`ExecutionEngine._positions`; see [the guardrails](/guardrails)).

The conviction floor does not apply: it asks how sure the model is of a
direction, and `NEUTRAL` names none. The fraction comes from the ticker's
kind in `config/instruments.py`, never from the signal.

<Note>
  0.5 is a starting point, not a measurement. Nothing in the journal can
  grade it yet, because no neutral hold had ever been opened. Grade it the
  way `MIN_CONVICTION` is graded: on the realised returns of the holds it
  produces.
</Note>

## 2. The core: idle money is held in three funds

Whatever the signal book leaves under the gross cap is invested, once a cycle
and after the last signal, in three funds no model is asked about:

| Fund | What it is | Bounded by |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `VT` | the world stock market — the benchmark the account is measured against | `MAX_EQUITY_RISK_PCT`, at beta 1.00, on top of the signal book's own net stock risk |
| `BND` | the US bond market | the Duration group's 30% ceiling, on top of the signal book's own duration |
| `SGOV` | Treasury bills, 0–3 months | the gross cap alone |

The gross room is handed out in that order. `CORE_EQUITY_SHARE` (60%) of it
is offered to `VT` and cut to the stock-market limit; what remains is offered
to `BND` and cut to the Duration ceiling; what remains after that is `SGOV`.
The three always sum to the gross room, and neither limit is exceeded to get
there. The core fills the limits that bound a loss. It never raises one.

### The core is the residual

Every limit a signal is sized under is computed on the book **without** the
core. A signal is sized as if the core were cash, so the core can never
refuse a signal its room. The same pass that buys the core sells it back
down when the signal book grows — sells first, then buys.

### The core is not a trade

A core fund has no stop and no ladder. It is the default allocation, and a
stop on a benchmark holding only sells it low to buy it back the next
morning. That makes it the one exception to "every entry is a bracket with a
stop", and the exception is kept narrow:

- `submit_core_order` can only **buy**, and only one of the three core funds.
  It refuses every other ticker inside `app/broker_client.py`, before the
  order is built.
- Only `app/core_book.py` calls it.
- The core funds are not on the watchlist. A shared ticker would merge a
  stopless holding with a signal position at the broker.
- The engine refuses a signal that names a core fund.
- The position manager drops the core funds from its list, so it never
  places a stop on one or walks one up the ladder.

CI checks the first three from the source.

### The band

A core fund is traded only when it is at least `CORE_REBALANCE_BAND_PCT` (1%)
of equity from its target, so a day's price moves are not a day's orders.
Buys are also cut to what the gross cap still allows after the sells, so no
order placed by the pass takes the account over it.

## How invested the account ends up

The gross cap is 95%. Whole-share rounding and the 1% band on each of three
funds can leave up to about 3% of that unfilled, so a cycle ends between
roughly **92% and 95% invested**.

Between cycles it can dip lower. A stop that fills during the session turns
that position into cash, and the cash is reinvested at the next cycle's core
pass, not the moment the stop fills.

## The order of a cycle

1. The [position manager](/position-management) walks the signal book.
2. Directional signals are dispatched as they are judged.
3. Neutral holds are dispatched, in watchlist order.
4. The core pass sells, then buys.

For a few seconds between steps 2 and 4 the account can be above the gross
cap: a signal is bought before the core is sold to make room for it. That
relies on the paper account being a margin account, which it is.

## Switching either off

Both are `Final` constants in `config/settings.py`, so turning one off is a
code change that shows up in review:

| Constant | Value | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `NEUTRAL_HOLD_ENABLED` | `False` | When on, `NEUTRAL` on a broad fund opens a long at its normal weight. Off until the core has run cleanly on its own. |
| `NEUTRAL_HOLD_FRACTION` | `0.5` | The normal weight, as a share of the fund's own cap. |
| `NEUTRAL_HOLD_SLEEVE_PCT` | `0.50` | Neutral holds stop filling the fund sleeve here. |
| `CORE_ENABLED` | `True` | Idle money is held in the core funds. |
| `CORE_EQUITY_SHARE` | `0.60` | Share of idle money offered to `VT` before the limits apply. |
| `CORE_REBALANCE_BAND_PCT` | `0.01` | The core's no-trade band, as a share of equity. |

With `CORE_ENABLED` off the pass does nothing — it does **not** sell what the
core already holds. Sell those three positions by hand if you want the cash
back.

## What does not model this yet

The simulated books in `shadow/` and `history/` build the engine with both
switches off, so they behave exactly as before. That keeps every registered
comparison unchanged, and it means the calibration copy no longer mirrors the
live account: it holds no core and opens no neutral holds. Expect it to
report those as differences until it is taught about them.

## Running the core pass by hand

```bash
python -m app.core_book          # one pass, human-readable
python -m app.core_book --json   # the same, as the cycle records it
```

It needs the market open, like every order here.
